# Documented Royal Descent — Eric Marion

A documented descent from medieval royalty, established via the
**gateway-ancestor method**: connecting a present-day person to a colonial
American immigrant whose royal pedigree has been peer-reviewed and badged by
the WikiTree Magna Carta Project (keyed to Douglas Richardson's *Magna Carta
Ancestry* / *Royal Ancestry*).

Found by matching a 32,507-person GEDCOM exported from FamilySearch via
RootsMagic against the 220 peer-reviewed Magna Carta gateway ancestors. One
high-confidence match (score 1.00 on name + birth year + birthplace + death
year); confirmed as a direct ancestor by walking the GEDCOM's FAMC links.

## The gateway

**Elizabeth (Mansfield) Wilson** — b. 1592 Henley-on-Thames, Oxfordshire,
England; d. 1658 Boston, Massachusetts Bay.

- WikiTree: [Mansfield-30](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Mansfield-30)
- FamilySearch: [27C7-LTR](https://www.familysearch.org/tree/person/details/27C7-LTR)
- Direct ancestor at **generation 13**

She emigrated to Massachusetts with her family in 1632 alongside her husband
**John Wilson Jr.**, who became pastor of the First Church of Boston.

## Segment B — the documented royal pedigree (badged by WikiTree's Magna Carta Project)

Elizabeth Mansfield's documented descent trails to **two of the 25 Magna Carta
surety barons**:

- **Robert FitzWalter** (~1180–1235) — [FitzWalter-101](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/FitzWalter-101). *Leader* of the baronial revolt against King John, styled "Marshal of the Army of God."
- **John FitzRobert of Clavering & Warkworth** (~1191–1240) — [Clavering-13](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Clavering-13). Lord of estates spanning Northumberland and Essex.

Both trails are peer-reviewed and "badged" by the project — i.e., the
gold-standard rigor in this field.

## Upstream royal ancestors

From the sureties, standard medieval royal genealogy carries upstream into:

- **Charlemagne** (742–814) — King of the Franks; Holy Roman Emperor 800
- **Rollo** (~860–~930) — 1st Duke of Normandy (911), founder of the Norman line
- **Malcolm III of Scotland** (~1031–1093) — King of Scots 1058–1093
- **William the Conqueror** (~1028–1087) — Duke of Normandy; King of England 1066–1087
- **Henry I of England** (1068–1135) — King of England 1100–1135
- **David I of Scotland** (~1084–1153) — King of Scots 1124–1153 (reached via the Earls of Dunbar)

Mildly ironic detail: the sureties forced *King John* — Henry I's
great-grandson — to seal the Magna Carta. So the descent runs through his
adversaries, not through John himself.

## Oldest well-documented ancestor

- **Confidently well-documented:** **Charlemagne (742–814)** — about 1,180
  years before me, ~40 generations. The conventional "deepest reliable" ancestor
  for any Magna Carta descendant.
- **Plausibly documented, accepted in standard pedigrees:** **Saint Arnulf of
  Metz** (~582–640) — Bishop of Metz, namesake ancestor of the Carolingian dynasty.
  ~1,350 years; the practical edge of *rigorous* Western European genealogy.
- **Cited but increasingly speculative:** **Clovis I** (~466–511) — founder of
  the Merovingian dynasty. ~1,500 years; treat as folklore-grade rather than proven.

## Caveats

The Mansfield match is a **high-confidence lead, not a proof.** Strict proof
still requires:

1. Verifying every parent-child link in Segment A above with primary records
   (vitals, wills, probates) — especially the colonial-to-English jump at gen
   11 → 12 (the immigrant generation), which is the most-attacked link in any
   such chain.
2. Confirming the Elizabeth Mansfield in my tree is *the same person* as
   WikiTree Mansfield-30 (already strongly supported by matching name, year,
   birthplace, *and* death year).

Once those are in hand, Segment B is inherited from the badged WikiTree trail
and Richardson's published work — that's the entire point of the
gateway-ancestor method.

A second possible independent line — **John Fisher b. 1585** (gen 13, 16-year
gap to gateway Fisher-6749) — is worth chasing as a follow-up but is not yet
confirmed.

### Biological vs. documented

By 30+ generations back, ancestral slots number in the billions and pedigree
collapse means most medieval European ancestors are shared with most other
Europeans. So "descended from Charlemagne" is biologically near-certain for
anyone with substantial European ancestry — what the gateway method
contributes is the *named, traceable path*, not the bare fact of descent.
